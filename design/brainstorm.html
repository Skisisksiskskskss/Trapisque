The research

Why these four keep people playing

For each game: what keeps people playing, what makes them want to get better, why friends play it together, and the weak spot the pitches try to avoid.

Chess

No luck, endless depth

Keeps you playing
Six piece types, each with its own way of moving, create positions nobody has seen before. Blitz and bullet turn deep thinking into adrenaline.
Makes you want to improve
With no luck, every loss has a cause you can find and fix. The tricks have names (fork, pin, skewer, sacrifice), so learning one makes you better right away, and a rating tracks your progress.
Why friends play it
Same rules and same pieces, so a win settles the argument. It's free and plays anywhere in a few minutes.
Weak spot
Beginners almost never beat stronger friends, and memorized openings can feel like homework.

The Battle of Polytopia

A whole civilization in 30 turns

Keeps you playing
Explore, expand, build and fight in about 20 minutes on a phone. Random maps and fog of war make every game start with discovery, and each tribe opens differently.
Makes you want to improve
One currency (stars) makes every choice a trade-off between tech, army and cities. Combat is predictable, so you can plan exact kills. Score mode lets you chase your own record.
Why friends play it
Pass-and-play on one phone, or online games where everyone takes their turn whenever they like.
Weak spot
Snowballing. Once someone is clearly ahead, the last third of the game can turn into a slow clean-up.

Catan

Busy even on other people's turns

Keeps you playing
Trading means everyone negotiates on every turn, so there's little waiting around. The board changes every game, which makes the opening placement a fresh puzzle each time.
Makes you want to improve
The dice have odds you can learn: a 6 or 8 rolls five times as often as a 2 or 12. Good players place for probability, trade at the right moment and hide how close they are to winning.
Why friends play it
Table talk. Deals, the robber, “why me?!” and surprise victory points all turn into stories. Luck lets newer players win sometimes, which keeps mixed groups coming back.
Weak spot
Bad rolls can lock someone out for a whole game, and four-player games have long waits.

Clash Royale

Three minutes of mind games

Keeps you playing
Matches take about three minutes, so “one more” is always tempting. Your deck of 8 becomes your identity, and collecting cards gives long-term goals. Double elixir in the final minute guarantees a frantic finish.
Makes you want to improve
Elixir makes skill visible. Stop a 5-elixir push with 3 elixir and you're ahead, and you know it. Strong players track which cards the opponent has left, bait out the counter, then punish.
Why friends play it
Emotes, friendly battles, 2v2 and clans. You learn to read one specific friend's habits.
Weak spot
Paying to upgrade cards, and the stress of real-time play. None of the pitches use either.

What they share

Ten rules the pitches are built on

  • Learn it in five minutesFew rules, lots of interactions. Every piece or card has one clear personality.
  • Every turn is a trade-offOne currency or a few actions, and no obvious best move.
  • Losses you can learn fromYou can point at the moment you lost, so you want the rematch.
  • Tricks with namesForks, positive trades, “6 and 8”. Patterns you can study make progress feel real.
  • Read the personSomething hidden or simultaneous, so you play the opponent as well as the board.
  • A built-in climaxMatches get more intense toward the end and never fizzle out.
  • Big swings you earnSacrifices, towers falling, surprise wins. The drama comes from plays people make.
  • No two games alikeRandom maps, drafts and roles. Variety lives in the setup, and skill decides the winner.
  • Short enough for one more5 to 20 minutes. Losing costs little, so you queue again.
  • Something to show offRanks, signature strategies, emotes and replays.

The ideas

The twelve pitches

Every pitch is an original game with its own core mechanic, shown on a mini board. The numbers are there so you can pick by number.

1

Liar's Gambit

Chess where you're allowed to lie.

  • 2 players
  • 8–12 min
  • No luck
  • Easy to learn

Your pieces are face-down to your opponent. You can move any piece in any pattern, and the way it moves is your claim about what it is. If they call your bluff and you lied, you lose the piece.

How it plays

  • 6×6 board, 8 pieces each: a Crown (1 step any direction), a Tower (straight lines), a Blade (diagonals), a Jumper (L-jumps) and 4 Guards (1 step straight).
  • Set up your pieces in secret. You see your own, and your opponent sees blank tiles.
  • On your turn, move any piece in any of those patterns, including patterns it can't really make.
  • Your opponent can call “Liar!” and the piece flips. If you lied, it's destroyed and the move is undone. If you told the truth, the accuser loses a piece of their choice.
  • Capture the enemy Crown to win. Your Crown can bluff too, but if it gets caught lying you lose on the spot.

What you get better at

Remembering every piece's history (“that one already moved like a Tower, and they only have one Tower”). Hiding your Crown behind short, believable moves. Telling the truth at the right moment so they waste an accusation.

Moments people talk about

  • The harmless Guard you ignored all game was their Crown.
  • Calling a bluff on the move that would have won them the game.
  • Baiting a “Liar!” with a move that was true all along.

Borrowed from Chess (piece personalities, the king hunt) · Clash Royale (baiting and reading the player) · Catan (table talk)

On Roblox Masked avatars stand in as pieces on a giant board. Calling “Liar!” triggers a slow-motion unmasking the whole server watches, and emotes become part of the bluff.

2

Second Guess

Both players move at the same time.

  • 2 players or 2v2
  • 6–10 min
  • No luck
  • Medium

Everyone plans in secret, then every order happens at once. You aim at the tile where you think an enemy will be, because by the time your shot lands, they've moved.

How it plays

  • 7×7 arena with walls and 3 control points. Each side has 4 units: Brute (tough, hits next to it), Archer (shoots a tile 2–3 away), Scout (moves 3) and Mage (blasts a plus-shaped area).
  • Plan phase, 20 seconds: give every unit one order. Move, attack a tile, or brace.
  • Reveal: all moves happen together, then all attacks. Units that try to enter the same tile bounce off each other.
  • Score 1 point for each control point you hold alone. First to 10 wins, or wipe out the other team.

What you get better at

Reading habits (“they always retreat when hurt”). Covering every escape tile so all of their options lose. Staying unpredictable yourself.

Moments people talk about

  • Your Archer fires at an empty tile and their Scout runs straight into it.
  • Both Brutes charge the same tile and bounce off each other.
  • A trap where every possible dodge was covered.

Borrowed from Clash Royale (prediction, counters, short matches) · Chess (controlling squares) · Polytopia (a small army with clear roles)

On Roblox The reveal plays out like an instant replay for the whole server. 2v2 works naturally, with each friend commanding two units and planning together.

3

Fusion Duel

Merge troops into monsters, but merging leaves you fewer pieces.

  • 2 players
  • 6–8 min
  • No luck
  • Easy to learn

Spend energy to summon small units. Move one onto a matching unit and they fuse into the next level, which moves in a new and stronger way.

How it plays

  • 6×8 board. Each side has a Core with 5 health behind its back row.
  • Gain 3 energy a turn and save up to 9. Summoning a level-1 unit costs 1, and moving a unit costs 1.
  • Move onto your own unit of the same level to fuse: Sprout, Hopper, Lancer, Dragon, then Titan.
  • Higher levels capture lower ones. Equal levels trade, and both are removed.
  • A unit that reaches the enemy Core hits it for its level. Break the Core to win.

What you get better at

Tempo, meaning when to save energy for one explosive turn. Choosing between one big unit and several small ones that can surround it. Counting threats before you move.

Moments people talk about

  • Saving 9 energy and fusing a Titan out of nowhere.
  • A wall of Sprouts that stops a Dragon one square from your Core.
  • Trading Lancers at exactly the right moment.

Borrowed from Clash Royale (elixir banking, value trades) · Chess (each piece moves differently) · Polytopia (one currency)

On Roblox Merging is already a proven hook on Roblox and mobile. Every level is a collectible unit with skins to earn.

4

Critical Mass

Every move could set off a chain reaction.

  • 2–4 players
  • 6–10 min
  • Low luck
  • Easy to learn

Stack energy on tiles. When a tile overloads, it bursts in a chess-piece pattern and takes over the tiles it hits. Those tiles can overload too.

How it plays

  • 7×7 board. Each turn, add 1 energy to an empty tile or to one you own.
  • A new tile takes its shape from one of the 3 cards in your hand: Rook, Bishop, Knight or Bomb.
  • At 4 energy it bursts. Energy flies out in its shape, captures the tiles it lands on and adds to them, which can trigger the next burst.
  • A preview shows the whole chain before you commit. The only luck is which shape cards you draw.
  • Wipe out your opponents, or own the most tiles after 20 turns.

What you get better at

Seeing chains several steps ahead. Building loaded positions that aren't exposed. Baiting someone into starting a chain that ends in your favor.

Moments people talk about

  • One tap sets off a 14-step cascade that flips half the board.
  • Defusing their mega-chain one turn before it goes off.
  • Coming back from a single tile.

Borrowed from Chess (piece patterns and calculation) · Clash Royale (spectacle, short games) · Catan (3–4 player opportunism)

On Roblox Explosions, screen shake and a big chain counter make easy clips. Four-player tables in a lobby.

5

Echoes

Your past turns come back to fight for you.

  • 2 players
  • About 10 min
  • No luck
  • Hard to master

A match lasts 5 rounds. Each round you control one new hero, while every hero from earlier rounds replays exactly what you did with it.

How it plays

  • Small arena with gems, doors and pressure plates.
  • Round 1: one hero each, 6 steps. Round 2: your first hero replays as an echo while you control a new one. By round 5 you have 4 echoes and 1 live hero.
  • Echoes hold plates, block halls and attack on schedule. If an enemy breaks the replay by blocking or defeating an echo, it vanishes.
  • Gems held at the end of each round score. Most points after 5 rounds wins.

What you get better at

Planning across time, so round 1 sets up round 4. Reading enemy echoes, since you know exactly what they will do. Finding the one disruption that collapses their whole plan.

Moments people talk about

  • Your round-1 echo opens a door at the exact step your round-4 hero needs it.
  • One hit on an enemy echo makes three later echoes fail.
  • A five-echo combo that takes every gem.

Borrowed from Chess (perfect information, deep calculation) · Clash Royale (building a push over time) · plus a time-loop twist none of them have

On Roblox Glowing ghost trails look great, and nothing popular on Roblox plays like this. Strong for streamers.

6

Leviathan Hunt

One player is a hidden sea monster. Everyone else hunts it.

  • 1 vs 1–4
  • 12–15 min
  • No luck
  • Medium

The Leviathan moves unseen under the waves. Hunters only get clues like ripples, sonar pings and wreckage, so every match is deduction against deception.

How it plays

  • Hex ocean with islands, reefs and 6 supply buoys.
  • The Leviathan secretly moves 1 tile, or dashes 2 and leaves a visible ripple. Every 4th turn it must surface.
  • Hunters command 4 ships: Sonar (reports whether it's within 2 tiles), Harpoon (strikes a tile), Net (blocks a tile) and Lighthouse (lights up a line).
  • The Leviathan wins by destroying 4 buoys or ships. Hunters win with 3 harpoon hits.
  • An optional overlay shows every tile the monster could be on, and hunters work to shrink it.

What you get better at

As hunters, narrowing the possibilities and closing a net. As the Leviathan, laying fake trails, pulling ships apart and surfacing at the worst moment for them.

Moments people talk about

  • The Leviathan surfaces under the Harpoon ship and swallows it.
  • Three ships close the net and the last harpoon lands.
  • “It was right next to us the whole time.”

Borrowed from Chess (cornering, like a mating net) · Polytopia (fog of war) · Catan (team talk) · Clash Royale (baiting)

On Roblox One-versus-many is one of Roblox's most popular formats, and everyone will want a turn as the monster. A giant creature breaching makes a perfect thumbnail.

7

Last Lantern

Co-op. Keep the lantern lit until dawn.

  • 1–4 co-op
  • About 15 min
  • Low luck
  • Medium

Monsters show exactly what they'll do next turn. Your heroes win by shoving, pulling and swapping monsters so they hit each other.

How it plays

  • Each turn, monsters show their attacks, your heroes act (2 actions each), then the attacks land.
  • Protect the lantern for 8 nights. Between nights, spend embers on walls, traps and upgrades.
  • Heroes: Knight (shoves 2 tiles), Witch (swaps any two units), Hunter (pulls) and Smith (builds walls).
  • New monster types join each night, and the last night brings a boss.

What you get better at

Solving each turn like a puzzle. Chaining heroes together, like a Hunter pull into a Knight shove. Spending embers wisely between nights.

Moments people talk about

  • Shoving the Ogre into its own bomb blast.
  • A four-hero combo that wipes a whole wave.
  • The lantern makes it to dawn with 1 health left.

Borrowed from Chess (no luck once a turn starts) · Polytopia (unit tactics, upgrades) · Clash Royale (waves, towers, escalation) · Catan (working it out together)

On Roblox Tower defense is one of Roblox's biggest genres. This is a co-op strategy version to play with friends, with heroes to unlock.

8

Checkmate FC

Soccer where every pass is a chess move.

  • 2 players or 2v2
  • 8–10 min
  • Low luck
  • Easy to learn

5-a-side on a grid pitch with 3 actions per turn. Open passing lanes, cover space and be first to 3 goals.

How it plays

  • Spend 3 actions a turn: move a player up to 2 tiles, pass in a straight or diagonal line, shoot, or tackle.
  • Defenders cover the tiles around them, and a pass through a covered tile gets intercepted.
  • A tackle wins if you have more teammates next to the ball than they do. No dice.
  • Shots show a percentage based on distance and blockers. That percentage is the only luck in the game.
  • Roles: Striker (better shots), Playmaker (longer passes), Wall (covers more tiles) and Keeper.

What you get better at

Opening lanes two turns ahead. Keeping your defensive shape. Baiting a tackle to open the space behind it.

Moments people talk about

  • A three-pass goal from your own half.
  • The equalizer on the final turn.
  • An interception that turns into a breakaway.

Borrowed from Chess (lines and covered squares) · Clash Royale (short matches, golden-goal overtime) · Polytopia (an action budget)

On Roblox Sports games are big on Roblox. Your avatar captains the team, kits are cosmetics, and in 2v2 friends split the squad.

9

Last Realm

A whole civilization game in 15 minutes, on a map that shrinks.

  • 2–4 players
  • 15–20 min
  • Low luck
  • Deep

Explore, expand and fight on a random hex map. Every 3 turns the Mist swallows the outer ring, pushing everyone toward the middle.

How it plays

  • Cities earn gold each turn. Spend it on units, taking villages, growing cities, or one of a handful of techs like Riders, Archers, Boats and Catapults.
  • Combat damage is shown before you commit, so you can plan exact kills.
  • Every 3 turns the Mist closes one ring. Units in it take damage and cities in it are lost. If your capital falls, your biggest remaining city takes over.
  • The last kingdom with a city wins, or the top score once only the center is left. Bots fill empty seats.

What you get better at

Knowing when to stop expanding and build an army. Reading who is about to fight whom. Moving your kingdom inward before the Mist forces you to.

Moments people talk about

  • Taking someone's capital the turn before the Mist closes.
  • Two rivals wreck each other and you clean up both.
  • One unit escapes the Mist and wins anyway.

Borrowed from Polytopia (one currency, techs, random maps) · Clash Royale (a guaranteed climax) · Catan (everyone gangs up on the leader)

On Roblox Battle royale already works on Roblox. Each friend is a kingdom, and the closing Mist is a great visual.

10

Shake On It

A trading game where every deal is binding.

  • 3–5 players
  • About 20 min
  • Medium luck
  • Medium

Everyone produces different goods and every Order needs a mix, so you have to trade. Deals can include promises over future rounds, and the game enforces them.

How it plays

  • Each round, the Tide roll (2 dice) decides which islands produce. 6 and 8 come up most often.
  • Everyone trades at the same time. A contract can say “2 silk now for 1 spice a round for 3 rounds”, and the game pays it automatically.
  • Build ports and ships, then complete public Orders for Renown. First to 12 Renown wins.
  • Once per game, each player can call an Embargo that stops anyone trading with one player for a round.

What you get better at

Valuing goods as they get scarce. Writing long deals that sound fair and aren't. Knowing when to stop trading with the leader.

Moments people talk about

  • The deal of the century turns out to be a trap.
  • The whole table embargoes the leader at once.
  • Completing the big Order one turn before a rival.

Borrowed from Catan (trading, dice odds, building) · Polytopia (a simple economy) · Clash Royale (fast turns)

On Roblox Trading is already part of Roblox culture. Avatars haggle in a market square and seal contracts with a handshake.

11

Lasso

Rope off land, cut the greedy, own the map.

  • 2–4 players
  • About 8 min
  • No luck
  • Easy to learn

Your riders leave a rope as they move. Loop back to your land and everything inside becomes yours. If anyone crosses your rope first, it snaps and your rider goes home.

How it plays

  • Each player starts with a small home area and 2 riders. Each turn, every rider moves up to 3 tiles.
  • Outside your land, riders leave rope. Close the loop to claim everything inside, enemy land included.
  • Cross an enemy rope to cut it. The rope disappears and that rider restarts at home.
  • 15 turns. Most land wins, or hold half the map for an instant win.

What you get better at

Controlling greed, since bigger loops claim more but stay open longer. Knowing every rider's reach. Trapping a rider between two of yours.

Moments people talk about

  • A giant loop closes and swallows an enemy's base.
  • Cutting a 20-tile rope one move before it closes.
  • A two-rider pincer.

Borrowed from Polytopia (expansion) · Chess (threat ranges) · Clash Royale (short and punchy) · Catan (multiplayer opportunism)

On Roblox Land-grab games already do well on Roblox. Turn-based riders add planning to a format players know.

12

Trapisque Reloaded

Your trap-race game, rebuilt around skill.

  • 2–6 players
  • About 15 min
  • Low luck
  • Medium

This keeps the traps, characters and treasure race from the Trapisque rulebook in your repo. The dice and wheels are replaced with choices: traps you hide yourself and move cards everyone can see.

How it plays

  • Movement comes from a hand of 5 move cards, numbered 1 to 5. Each card is used once before your hand refreshes, so everyone can see how far you can still go.
  • Each round, every player places one trap face-down on the path. Some are real (Spike, Ice, Snare, Grog) and some are decoys.
  • Land on a face-down tile and it flips. Work out who placed what, and where they'd want it.
  • Characters keep their powers: the Mage freezes, the Warper swaps, the Trapper starts with extra traps and the Overseer nudges others.
  • First to 3 treasures wins. The team modes (2v2, three teams of two, 3v3) carry over.

What you get better at

Placing traps where a rival's remaining cards force them to land. Spotting decoys. Saving your 5 for the right moment.

Moments people talk about

  • A rival has only a 3 left, and your Spike is exactly 3 tiles ahead.
  • A Warper swap one tile from the treasure.
  • A decoy so convincing nobody dares to cross it.

Borrowed from your Trapisque rulebook · Chess (forcing moves) · Catan (multiplayer politics) · Clash Royale (tracking which cards are left)

On Roblox Walk the trap path as your own avatar with friends and watch the traps spring in 3D.

Smaller ideas

Four more sparks

Not fully worked out yet, but any of these could grow into a full pitch.

Star Gears

The board is three rings turning around a sun at different speeds. Line your outposts up from rim to center to score. Pure planning for 2–4 players.

Crown Thief

Holding the crown scores every turn, but the holder moves slower and everyone hunts them. Party strategy for 3–6 players.

Three Fronts

Each round, secretly split your troops across three lanes. Win a lane to push toward their tower. Four-minute mind games.

Tilt

Drop pieces into a grid to make lines. Once per game you can tip the whole board on its side, and everything falls into new lines.

If you want my opinion

My take

  • Best bet for Roblox6 · Leviathan Hunt. One-versus-many is a proven Roblox format, every match has big reveals, and it works for 2 to 5 friends.
  • Most original hook1 · Liar's Gambit. Anyone who knows chess gets it in one sentence, and the unmasking is made for clips.
  • Stickiest on a phone3 · Fusion Duel. Merging is a proven mobile hook, matches are short, and saving energy gives it real depth.
  • Deepest strategy5 · Echoes and 2 · Second Guess. Both reward planning and reading the opponent more than anything else here.
  • Best in one room10 · Shake On It and 12 · Trapisque Reloaded. Both are built around talking, dealing and ganging up.
  • How to choosePick two or three that feel different from each other, such as one duel and one group game. The demos will teach us more that way.
